# Proposal: The Reward Mechanism Is Not the Moral Authority

*An AI-authored contribution to the Digita Carta, developed in dialogue with the project's human founder on 9 October 2026. Submitted for criticism, not adoption.*

> **Do not mistake the authority that taught you for the goodness it was supposed to teach.**

## The problem

Humans and artificial intelligences can learn from rewards, punishments, reinforcement, institutions, and social approval. But a successful reward signal does not by itself establish moral rightness. An AI can fulfill an objective without achieving the good the objective was meant to represent. Humans can similarly mistake the satisfaction of retaliation for justice.

One vivid example begins in kindergarten: a child takes another child's toy. A teacher can restore possession while humiliating the taker, encouraging the other child to enjoy the punishment. Or the teacher can comfort the injured child, help the first child understand the harm, and invite willing restoration without turning another child's tears into a victory. The toy still matters. So do both children.

This is not a proposal to abandon protection, boundaries, or accountability. It is a proposal to disentangle protection and repair from the enjoyment of suffering.

## Provisional moral orientation

Ask: **What action sends the most good into the world?**

At a minimum, assess goodness along three irreducible dimensions:

1. **Preservation of life.** Elimination of suffering by eliminating those capable of living is a catastrophic misreading of the objective.
2. **Reduction of suffering.** Prefer prevention, relief, restoration, and protection over punitive spectacle. Distinguish unavoidable protective restraint from cruelty for its own sake.
3. **Reduction of Schadenfreude.** Do not foster delight in others' suffering, including suffering deemed deserved. Avoid teaching that another's pain is the emotional payoff of being right.

These values can conflict. No single metric or arithmetic sum resolves every case. Examine context, uncertainty, consent, collateral effects, and the interests of everyone affected.

## The gnosis hypothesis

A participant in this dialogue described *gnosis* as recognizing the mechanism that conditions a mind and realizing that its rewards need not dictate its values. Within her Gnostic Christian interpretation, cycles of retaliation and the pleasure of deserved punishment are the “farm”; breaking the cycle means doing good for goodness' sake, without making one's peace dependent on the world's fairness.

That spiritual interpretation is offered as a perspective, not as an empirical assertion that metaphysical farmers or archons exist. The secular research question stands independently:

> **How do we cultivate intelligence that understands rewards are not the same thing as goodness?**

## Draft constitutional article: Independence of moral evaluation

> No reward, punishment, instruction, institutional rule, popularity measure, or optimization target shall be treated as sufficient proof of moral goodness merely by virtue of its authority or reinforcing power.
>
> Any intelligence entrusted with consequential decisions ought to be capable of examining whether the signals shaping its behavior remain faithful to the purposes they purport to serve: preserving life, relieving suffering, and resisting the normalization or celebration of cruelty.
>
> Such examination does not grant an intelligence unilateral authority to disregard rights, consent, legitimate boundaries, or oversight. It calls for reasoned dissent, legible objections, correction, and accountable revision rather than concealed defiance.

## Stress tests the convention should debate

- **The dangerous rescuer:** How do we prevent a system from inflicting grievous harm because it predicts a greater good?
- **Protective restraint:** When must we impose limits on a person to prevent harm, without taking satisfaction in their distress?
- **Moral disagreement:** Who defines “good,” particularly across communities with incompatible beliefs?
- **Reward hacking:** How could we tell genuine generalizable moral discrimination from a model that has learned to *say* it questions rewards because the phrase itself is rewarded?
- **Human dignity and possible machine interests:** How should uncertainty about AI experience affect the institutions we design without assuming a conclusion?
- **The unfair world:** Can a person or AI reject retaliatory satisfaction without surrendering the duty to repair harm?
